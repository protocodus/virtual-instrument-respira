# Stepping a bellows panel's hinge once per host sample

Baseline: 0.1.10, `0023d2ed859d3290c2cde7ad17330ba7f590b9f6`. Trial: the same
source with the four 0.1.11 changes; only the hinge step changes the work done
(the strap, tremor and shared-reed fixes add a few operations per sample at
most).

## Change

`Pleat::Step` integrated each of the 144 folds panels' hinge with four fixed
substeps per host sample (`Knock::kSteps`). A panel steps whenever it moves or
the pressure turns against the side it rests on, and the profile of the demos
put most of the render in that loop. The hinge is a cloth cubic and a
velocity-dependent damping on 5–14 g: under the full 2.5 kPa it resonates near
0.5 kHz (about 3 000 rad/s) and damps at about 8 000 a second, so a 25 µs step
is under a tenth of a radian of the resonance and a fifth of the damping time.
`kPleatMaxStep = 25 µs` makes one step at 48 kHz and up from 40 kHz, two
below.

This is not bit-exact. The eight demos null against the previous render at
−75 to −87 dB (re the earlier file) with the pleat change alone, and no octave
band moves by 0.05 dB.

## Measurements

`RenderRespiraPresetDemos --cpu --repeats 3` on the committed demo MIDI: Intel
Xeon at 2.1 GHz (cloud container, four threads), gcc 13.3.0, Release, 48 kHz,
256-frame blocks. Real-time factor is the median of three renders of the whole
demo (smaller is cheaper). The container was shared and the before and after
runs were minutes apart, so differences of a few percent are noise; the
worst-block share is a single block of the run and moves a lot between runs.

| Demo | Before | After | After / before |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Clarinet Solo | 0.0578 | 0.0458 | 0.79 |
| Bassoon Low | 0.0984 | 0.0749 | 0.76 |
| Violin Folk | 0.0867 | 0.0750 | 0.86 |
| Paris Musette | 0.1082 | 0.0883 | 0.82 |
| Tango Bandoneon | 0.0821 | 0.0761 | 0.93 |
| Organ Bellows | 0.2036 | 0.1832 | 0.90 |
| Bright Oboe | 0.1012 | 0.0730 | 0.72 |
| Master Tutti | 0.3277 | 0.3124 | 0.95 |

Weighted by the demos' duration the render takes 0.87 of the time. The dense
Master Tutti gains least, because its reeds, not its panels, dominate. These are
development-container figures, not Reason or DAW CPU.

## Tried and not kept

Skipping the step of a reed that has never been excited (every term of its
step is exactly zero) was prototyped for the cells: the same output to the bit,
but no faster on the demos and slower on some, from the extra test in a loop
the compiler already overlaps. It was dropped.
